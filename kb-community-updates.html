How community updates work

Notices, announcements, alerts, news, events and polls — how the office drafts, publishes and takes them down for residents.

On this page

  6. Polls

This guide is for the managing committee and the office staff who publish news to residents. A community update is a notice, an announcement, an alert, a piece of news, an event, or a poll. Residents see published updates in their feed and on the home screen. The office keeps the draft until it is ready to send.

An update belongs to one or more societies on the company. It must name at least one society before it can be saved. Residents of those societies are the people who can open it. A resident of another society on the same company does not see it in the feed.

Outgoing email for the bill PDF is a separate switch, described in How to onboard a property on Jameen. That same switch decides whether publishing an update also emails residents.

What an update is made of

FieldWhat the office enters
TitleThe line residents see first. Required.
BodyThe full text. It can be formatted. Pictures and layout in the body stay in the update itself.
Short textThe plain sentence used in the notification and the email. If the office leaves this blank, Jameen takes the opening of the body, up to 500 characters. A short text the office writes is kept as they wrote it.
CategoryNotice, announcement, event, alert, news, or poll. The category decides whether the update is a plain message, an event people can answer, or a poll people can vote in.
SocietiesEvery society that should receive it. At least one.
AudienceWho inside those societies may open it. See Who can see it.
PriorityNormal, important, or urgent. This is a label on the update. It does not move the update up the feed. Pinning does that.
CommentsOff when the update is created. Turn comments on when residents should reply under it.
Ask residents to confirm they have read itA mark on the update. Jameen does not keep a list of who confirmed.
Publish dateThe date used to order the feed and to show when the update was issued. Publishing sends the update at the moment the office publishes it. A future publish date does not hold the update back.
ExpiresThe moment the update leaves the resident feed. Leave it blank and the update stays until the office unpublishes it. If both a publish date and an expiry are set, the expiry must be later than the publish date.
PinKeeps the update at the top of the feed.
Pin untilWhen the pin lets go. Leave it blank and the pin stays until the office removes it. A pin whose time has passed no longer floats to the top. The update itself remains in the feed until it expires or is unpublished.

An alert is the category that goes out as a high-priority security notice. Marking a notice or an announcement as urgent labels it. It does not, by itself, raise the notification to that high priority. Use the alert category for water shutdowns, gate closures, and anything residents should treat as urgent.

Draft, publish, and take down

A new update is a draft. Residents do not see a draft, and they are not notified.

Publish sends it.

  • The update becomes visible in the feed of residents who match the societies and the audience, until it expires.
  • Those residents are notified. The notification title is the update’s title. The notification text is the short text.
  • Where outgoing email is on for a selected society, residents of that society are emailed the title and the name of the person who published it.
  • The three newest updates also appear on the resident’s home screen.

Unpublish returns the update to draft. It leaves the resident feed. Residents are not sent a second message to say it was withdrawn. The office can then edit it and publish it again.

A published update cannot be edited and cannot be deleted. To change the wording, unpublish it, edit the draft, and publish again. To remove it for good, unpublish it first, then delete the draft. Deleting an event or a poll also deletes the replies and the votes.

The office list still shows drafts, published updates, and updates that have expired. Expiry hides an update from residents. It does not delete it.

Who can see it

The feed shows an update only when all of these are true.

  • It is published.
  • It has not expired.
  • The resident belongs to one of the societies on the update.
  • The resident matches the audience.
AudienceWho can open it
All residentsEvery resident of the selected societies. This is the starting choice.
Household headsOwners, co-owners, and primary residents. An owner is included even when the flat has no separate primary resident, so an owner-occupied flat is not skipped.
Women residentsResidents whose gender is recorded as female.
Adults onlyResidents with a date of birth at least 18 years ago. A resident with no date of birth on file is left out.

A resident who does not match the audience does not get the update when they open the feed. Choose the audience before publishing. After publishing, changing the audience means unpublishing, editing, and publishing again.

Write a restricted update with care. Publishing notifies residents, and the email goes to residents of the selected societies where outgoing email is on. The feed is what limits who can open the update afterwards. Do not put private detail in an update on the assumption that only the chosen audience will hear that it exists.

Pinned updates sit above the rest. After the pins, the feed is newest publish date first. An update with no publish date follows those that have one, and is then ordered by when it was created.

Notices, announcements, alerts, and news

These four categories are messages. They have a title and a body, and they follow the draft and publish rules above. They do not ask for an RSVP and they do not take a vote.

Use them this way.

CategoryA typical use
NoticeA standing item: office hours, a rule, a document residents should keep.
AnnouncementSomething new: a meeting, a charge, a festival plan.
AlertSomething residents should act on soon. This is the category sent as a high-priority notice.
NewsA report of something that has happened: a completed repair, a result, a thank-you.

Comments, when the office has turned them on, are written replies from residents. Each reply stores the resident’s name and the text. The office can read the replies on the update and can remove one. A reply needs text. It cannot be blank.

Events

Choose the event category when residents should be able to say whether they are coming.

An event can be saved as a draft before the schedule is filled in. It cannot be published until the schedule exists.

FieldWhat to enter
StartsRequired. The date and time the event begins.
EndsOptional. If set, it must be after the start.
PlaceWhere residents should come.
Notes on the placeChairs, parking, what to bring.
CapacityHow many seats. Leave it blank for no limit. A number must be at least 1.
RSVP byThe last moment a resident can answer. It cannot be after the start. Leave it blank and answers stay open until the event starts.

A resident answers going, not going, or maybe. They may bring guests. The guest count is a whole number from 0 to 20. One answer is allowed per resident. Changing the answer updates that same answer. They can also withdraw it.

Seats are counted only for people who are going: the resident, plus the guests on that answer. Maybe and not going do not take a seat. If a capacity is set, a going answer is refused when it would take more seats than are left. A resident who is already going can change their own guest count. Their old seats are not counted twice.

Answers close when any of these is true.

  • The RSVP deadline has passed.
  • There is no deadline, and the start time has passed.
  • The end time has passed, or the start time has passed when no end time was set.

The office can open the attendance list and a summary: how many are going, maybe, and not going, how many guests, how many seats are taken, how many are left, and whether answers are still open. When there is no capacity, seats left is not shown.

The schedule can be edited only while the update is a draft. Unpublish the event before changing the time or the place, then publish it again. Answers already given remain unless the update is deleted.

Polls

Choose the poll category when residents should vote.

A poll can be saved as a draft before the choices are finished. It cannot be published until it has a closing time and enough choices.

FieldWhat to enter
KindOne choice, several choices, or a rating.
ChoicesThe options, in the order residents should see them. A one-choice or several-choice poll needs at least two. Each label can be up to 120 characters.
AnonymousWhen this is on, the ballot is not stored against the resident’s name.
ClosesRequired, and it must be in the future when the poll is created. After this moment residents cannot vote.
Show results before it closesWhen this is on, residents see the running counts while the poll is still open. When it is off, residents see the counts only after the closing time. The office always sees the counts.

A rating poll is a scale from 1 to 5. If the office does not type the five labels, Jameen creates them. A rating answer is one number on that scale.

How a resident votes:

KindWhat they may select
One choiceExactly one option.
Several choicesOne or more options.
RatingExactly one step on the scale.

A resident who is named on the ballot can change their vote until the poll closes. The new vote replaces the old one. A resident on an anonymous poll cannot change their vote, because the ballot is not kept in their name. They also cannot vote a second time. Jameen still records that they have voted, so the poll can be closed to them, without storing which option they picked.

The office sees each option’s votes and its share of the votes. On a poll that is not anonymous, the office can also open the list of who chose what. On an anonymous poll that list is empty. The count of people who voted is still there.

After the first vote:

  • a choice that already has votes cannot be removed
  • the kind of poll cannot be changed

To change the question, unpublish the poll, edit the draft, and publish again. Votes already cast stay on the poll unless the update is deleted.

A vote is refused when the poll has not been published, when the closing time has passed, or when the selection does not match the kind of poll.

What residents see

On the home screen, the three newest updates that this resident is allowed to see.

In the feed, every published update for their societies that has not expired and that matches their audience. Pinned updates first, then the newest publish date.

Opening an update shows the body, and, when they apply, the event answer or the poll. A resident who is not allowed to see an update cannot open it by guessing at it.

They can:

  • comment, when they write a reply
  • answer an event, change that answer, or withdraw it, while answers are open and seats remain
  • vote in a poll while it is open, and change that vote when the poll is not anonymous

They cannot edit the update, publish it, or see a draft.

A short example

The office at Horizon Residency prepares a water shutdown.

StepWhat happens
DraftCategory alert. Title “Water shutdown, Tower A”. Body with the timings. Society: Horizon Residency. Audience: all residents. Pin on, until the evening the water returns. Expiry the morning after. Comments left off.
PublishResidents of Horizon Residency are notified, with the alert sent at high priority. The update sits at the top of their feed. If outgoing email is on, they are also emailed the title.
During the shutdownThe pin keeps it above later news.
After the pin timeIt stays in the feed, no longer pinned.
After the expiryIt leaves the feed. The office can still open it.

A Republic Day flag hoisting is an event instead: start and end on the lawn, capacity 80, RSVP the evening before. Residents answer going, maybe, or not going. The office watches seats left.

A clubhouse colour is a poll: one choice, two colours, not anonymous, results hidden until it closes. Residents can change their mind until the closing time. The office can see who chose which colour. After the closing time, residents see the counts too.

What the office should remember

  • A draft is private. Residents hear about an update only when it is published, and they hear about it then, not on a future publish date.
  • A published update is locked. Unpublish it to edit or to delete it. Unpublishing does not send a withdrawal message.
  • The societies on the update decide whose feed it enters. The audience narrows that further: everyone, household heads, women, or adults. An adult is someone with a date of birth at least 18 years ago.
  • Pinning lifts an update to the top. Priority labels it. The alert category is what raises the notification.
  • Expiry removes it from the feed and leaves it in the office list.
  • An event needs a start time before it can be published. Seats count people who are going, plus their guests. Answers close at the RSVP deadline, or at the start when no deadline was set.
  • A poll needs a closing time and at least two choices before it can be published. Anonymous votes cannot be changed and cannot be traced to a name. The office always sees the counts.
  • The “please confirm you have read this” mark does not produce a list of who confirmed.
