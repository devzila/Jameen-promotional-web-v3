How Jameen raises recurring maintenance bills

This guide is for the managing committee, the treasurer, and the society’s accountant. It explains when a maintenance bill is raised on its own, which months are billing months, what period the bill covers, when the resident must pay, when the bill becomes overdue, and how a late fee is added.

A recurring bill is the society’s regular maintenance invoice. Jameen raises it for each allotment on the billing day. The office does not have to create these bills one flat at a time.

The books behind the bill — the receivable, GST on the bill, a waiver, and a payment — are described in How Jameen keeps the society’s accounts.

This guide is the operating rule inside Jameen. It does not replace the society’s bye-laws or a resolution of the general body on the rate, the due date, or the late fee.

The settings that decide the cycle

These settings live on the property. They apply to every allotment in that society. One flat cannot have a different billing month or a different late-fee rule from the flat next door.

SettingWhat the office choosesWhat it changes
Billing frequencyMonthly, every two months, quarterly, half-yearly, or yearlyHow often a bill is raised, how many months one bill covers, and how the maintenance amount is multiplied
Billing start monthA calendar month, from January to December. The default is AprilWhich months are billing months. It does not change the dates printed on the bill
In arrears or in advanceIn arrears (after the period) or in advance (before the period)Whether the bill covers the months just finished or the months about to start
Invoice generation dayA day of the month, from 1 to 31. The default is the 1stThe calendar day on which the bills are raised
Days to payA number of days after the bill date. The default is 14The due date. The bill becomes overdue the day after that date
Late-fee ruleNone, a flat amount once, a percentage once, or an amount each dayWhether, and how, a late fee is added after the due date
GST rate on the propertyA percent. Zero means no GST on maintenanceGST on the maintenance line
Outgoing emailOn or off. On is the defaultWhether the resident is emailed a PDF when the bill is raised

Billing has to be switched on for the society, and both the society and the company have to be active. If either is inactive on the generation day, that cycle is not raised.

When a bill is raised

Jameen looks at every society early each morning. A society is billed that morning only when all of the following are true.

  1. The company is active, the society is active, and billing is on.
  2. Today is the invoice generation day. In a short month, a generation day of 29, 30, or 31 falls on the last day of that month. The detail is in The invoice generation day.
  3. This calendar month is a billing month for the frequency and the billing start month. The detail is in Which months are billing months.
  4. The allotment does not already have a bill whose dates overlap the period this run would cover.

When those are true, Jameen raises one bill for each allotment on the property. The bill date is that calendar day. The period printed on the bill is calculated from the rules below. The due date is the bill date plus the days to pay.

The resident on the allotment receives a notification that the bill is ready. If outgoing email is on, they also receive an email with the bill PDF.

A morning that fails any of the checks above does nothing for that society. Jameen does not try again the next day. The next chance is the next billing month, on the invoice generation day.

A bill the office raises by hand is a separate action. It uses the dates and the lines the office types in, and it is limited to allotments that are still running. It is not this schedule.

Who receives a scheduled bill

The schedule follows the allotment, which is the ownership record of the flat.

  • Every allotment on the property receives a bill, including an allotment whose end date has already passed. Closing the allotment does not, by itself, stop the next scheduled bill.
  • A moving-in stay is not billed by this schedule. The people who have moved in are not a second bill.
  • The schedule does not look at the date the allotment started. A flat allotted in the last month of a quarter still receives the full quarter’s charge on the billing day.
  • A bill to someone who is not a member, such as an advertiser, is never raised by this schedule and never receives an automatic late fee.

If the same allotment already has a bill that overlaps the new period, that flat is skipped. The other flats on the society are still billed.

Which months are billing months

The billing frequency and the billing start month answer one question: is this month a month in which bills are raised?

The start month is the anchor. Count forward by the length of the frequency, and those are the billing months. The start month itself is always a billing month.

Anchor in April, which is the default and matches a society that thinks in the Indian financial year:

FrequencyLength of one billBilling months
Monthly1 monthEvery month
Every two months2 monthsApril, June, August, October, December, February
Quarterly3 monthsApril, July, October, January
Half-yearly6 monthsApril, October
Yearly12 monthsApril only

Anchor in January, the same frequencies shifted:

FrequencyBilling months
MonthlyEvery month
Every two monthsJanuary, March, May, July, September, November
QuarterlyJanuary, April, July, October
Half-yearlyJanuary, July
YearlyJanuary only

Any other start month works the same way. A July anchor with quarterly billing uses July, October, January, and April.

A month that is not in that list is a quiet month. The generation day can fall in a quiet month and Jameen will still raise nothing. Example: quarterly billing anchored in April, generation day the 5th. On 5 May, 5 June, 5 August, and so on, no bills are raised. Bills are raised on 5 April, 5 July, 5 October, and 5 January.

Monthly billing has no quiet month. Only the generation day, and whether the flat already has an overlapping bill, decide the run.

Changing the start month moves the list of billing months. It does not rewrite bills already raised. The next run uses the new list.

What period the bill covers

The billing start month chooses the month of the run. It does not choose the dates printed on the bill. Those dates come from the month the bills are raised, the length of the frequency, and whether the society bills in arrears or in advance.

The period is always whole calendar months. It starts on the 1st and ends on the last day of a month.

In arrears. The bill covers the period that has just finished. The last day of the period is the last day of the month before the billing month. The period then runs backward for the length of the frequency.

In advance. The bill covers the period that is just starting. The first day of the period is the 1st of the billing month. The period then runs forward for the length of the frequency.

The same society, billed on 5 April 2026:

FrequencyIn arrears, the bill coversIn advance, the bill covers
Monthly1 March 2026 – 31 March 20261 April 2026 – 30 April 2026
Every two months1 February 2026 – 31 March 20261 April 2026 – 31 May 2026
Quarterly1 January 2026 – 31 March 20261 April 2026 – 30 June 2026
Half-yearly1 October 2025 – 31 March 20261 April 2026 – 30 September 2026
Yearly1 April 2025 – 31 March 20261 April 2026 – 31 March 2027

A yearly bill in arrears, raised in April, is the financial year that has just ended. A yearly bill in advance, raised in April, is the financial year that is just starting. That is why the default start month is April. A society that wants the yearly bill in January sets the start month to January. The period then follows January, not April.

The bill date and the due date are not the period dates. A bill raised on 5 April for January–March is dated 5 April. The resident’s time to pay is counted from 5 April.

The invoice generation day

The invoice generation day is the date of the bill. Early that morning, Jameen raises the bills and dates them that day.

If the day is the 5th, bills are dated the 5th of each billing month. They are not dated the 1st, and they are not dated the last day of the period.

A generation day of 29, 30, or 31 cannot fall in every month. On the last day of a shorter month, Jameen treats that day as the generation day for every setting from that day through the 31st.

MonthLast dayA generation day of 31 is raised on
January3131 January
April3030 April
February, not a leap year2828 February
February, a leap year2929 February

So a society that picks the 31st is billed on the last day of every billing month. A society that picks the 15th is billed on the 15th only. The 15th exists in every month, so there is no shift.

If the society is inactive, or billing is off, on the morning of the generation day, that cycle is missed. Jameen does not raise it on the 16th because the 15th was missed. The office raises those bills by hand, or waits for the next billing month.

How the amount is calculated

The amount follows the billing frequency. It is the charge for the full number of months in that frequency. It is not reduced because the resident joined late, and it is not reduced because a previous bill already covered some of the same months. The protection against charging twice is to skip the flat when the periods overlap, which is described above.

Maintenance

Each unit type has a maintenance rate per square foot, and each unit has an area.

Maintenance for the bill = rate per square foot × area × number of months in the frequency.

FrequencyMonths used in the multiplication
Monthly1
Every two months2
Quarterly3
Half-yearly6
Yearly12

Example. The rate is ₹4 per square foot. The flat is 1,000 square feet. One month of maintenance is ₹4,000. A quarterly bill is ₹12,000. A yearly bill is ₹48,000.

GST on that line is the property’s GST rate applied to the maintenance amount. A rate of zero means the line has no GST. A rate of 18% on ₹12,000 adds ₹2,160, and the maintenance line total is ₹14,160.

Other charges on the unit type

Each active extra charge on the unit type is a further line. An inactive charge is left off.

  • A fixed charge is its monthly amount × the same number of months. GST is that charge’s own GST rate.
  • A percentage charge is that percent of the maintenance amount, before GST. GST is then that charge’s own GST rate.

Example. A fixed parking charge of ₹500 a month on a quarterly bill is ₹1,500, plus GST at the rate set on that charge. A sinking-fund charge set at 10% of maintenance, on maintenance of ₹12,000, is ₹1,200, plus GST at the rate set on that charge.

The bill the resident must pay is the sum of the lines, including GST. That total is the figure posted to Resident Receivable, as described in the accounting guide.

The line description on the maintenance charge states the rate, the area, and the number of months, so the resident can see how the figure was built.

The due date

The due date is the bill date plus the days to pay.

The default is 14 days. A bill dated 5 April is due on 19 April. A bill dated 31 January with 10 days to pay is due on 10 February.

The due date is a day the resident may still pay. The bill is on time through the end of that date.

The days to pay are counted from the bill date, which is the generation day. They are not counted from the end of the period the bill covers. A bill raised on 5 April for January–March, with 14 days to pay, is due on 19 April, not on 14 April and not on 31 March.

Changing the days to pay changes the due date of bills raised after the change. A bill already raised keeps the due date it was given.

When a bill becomes overdue

Jameen does not store a separate “overdue” status. A bill is overdue when both of these are true:

  • the due date is before today, and
  • the bill is still unpaid, or only partly paid.
Bill dateDays to payDue dateFirst day the unpaid bill is overdue
5 April1419 April20 April
1 April01 April2 April
30 April77 May8 May

A bill that is fully paid is not overdue, even if it was paid after the due date. A cancelled bill is not overdue. A bill that has been written off is not overdue.

A partly paid bill stays overdue until the bill and any late fees on it are fully settled. Paying half on the due date does not reset the clock.

The office can filter the bill list for overdue bills. That filter uses the same rule: due date already passed, and the bill is unpaid or partly paid.

How the late fee is calculated

If the society has chosen a late-fee rule other than “none”, Jameen adds the late fee on its own. It does this early each morning, after the billing run, for every active society that has a late-fee rule.

A late fee is a debit note on that same bill. It has its own amount still outstanding. The resident owes the unpaid bill plus any open late fees. A payment is applied to the bill first and then to the late fees, as described in the accounting guide. In the books the late fee is Late Fee Income.

A late fee is added only when all of the following are true.

  • The bill is a member bill (an allotment bill). A bill to a non-member is never charged a late fee by this job.
  • The bill is overdue: the due date is before today, and the bill is unpaid or partly paid.
  • The bill is not cancelled and not fully paid.
  • The late-fee amount works out to more than zero.
  • That particular late fee has not already been added. The four rules below say what “already” means.

The late fee is calculated on the morning the bill is first overdue, and, for the daily rule, on each following morning the bill is still open.

No late fee

Nothing is added, however long the bill stays open.

A flat amount, once

The society sets one rupee amount, for example ₹500. On the first morning the bill is overdue, Jameen adds one debit note for ₹500, with the reason “Late payment penalty”.

Later mornings do not add another ₹500, even if the bill is still open, and even if the resident has paid the ₹500 and still owes the original bill. The flat fee is once for the life of that bill.

The amount does not depend on the size of the bill or on how many days late it is.

A percentage, once

The society sets a percent, for example 2. On the first morning the bill is overdue, Jameen adds one debit note for that percent of the bill total.

The bill total is the original invoice total, including GST. It is not the unpaid balance.

Example. The bill total is ₹12,000. The rule is 2%. The late fee is ₹240, once. If the resident has already paid ₹10,000 and ₹2,000 is still open, the late fee is still ₹240, because 2% is taken from ₹12,000.

The percentage is rounded to paise. Later mornings do not add it again.

An amount each day

The society sets a rupee amount per day, for example ₹20. Each morning the bill is still overdue, Jameen adds one debit note for ₹20. The reason names that calendar date, for example “Late payment penalty for 20 April 2026”.

MorningBill still open and overdueWhat is added
20 AprilYes₹20 for 20 April
21 AprilYes₹20 for 21 April
22 AprilThe bill was paid on 21 AprilNothing

The fee for a day is added once. A day that has already been charged is not charged again.

The job does not go back and fill in days it did not run. Each morning adds that morning’s amount only.

A partly paid bill keeps receiving a daily note until the bill is fully paid or cancelled. Paying the late fees already on the bill, while the original bill is still open, does not stop the next morning’s note.

What the late fee does not do

  • It does not change the original bill total. The bill stays ₹12,000, and the late fee sits beside it.
  • It does not start on the due date. The due date is still a day to pay. The first late fee is on the next morning.
  • It is not GST. The debit note is the late-fee amount only.
  • It is not added to a non-member bill.

A late fee the office wants to forgive is handled as a settlement of that debit note, in the same way as any other amount on the bill. The original bill is a separate balance.

How a change in the settings affects the next cycle

Settings are read on the morning of the run. A change applies to bills not yet raised. Bills already on the ledger keep their date, their period, their amount, and their due date.

What the office changesWhat the next run does
Billing frequencyUses the new length for the billing months, for the period, and for the multiplication of the rate. A quarterly society that becomes monthly is billed every month, for one month, at one month’s maintenance
Billing start monthMoves the list of billing months. The period rule is unchanged: the period still follows the month of the run, the frequency, and arrears or advance
Arrears or advanceThe next bill covers the past period or the coming period, using the new choice
Invoice generation dayThe next bills are dated the new day. A day that has already passed this month is not rerun
Days to payThe next bills get a due date from the new number of days
Late-fee rule or amountThe next morning’s late-fee pass uses the new rule. A flat or percentage fee already added to an open bill is not added a second time. A daily rule uses the new daily amount from the next morning
Maintenance rate, area, or an extra chargeThe next bill uses the new figures. A charge marked inactive is left off the next bill
GST rateThe next bill uses the new rate

Two further effects are worth planning for.

A missed generation day stays missed. Switching billing back on the day after the generation day does not produce that month’s bills. The next automatic bills are on the generation day of the next billing month.

An overlapping period is not billed again. If the society shortens the frequency, or moves from advance to arrears, a flat that already has a bill covering any part of the new period is skipped. The other flats are billed. The amount on a bill that is raised is still the full frequency — rate × area × the new number of months — even when the resident’s earlier bill covered a neighbouring month that does not overlap.

Example. The society was monthly, in arrears, and the May bill covered 1–31 May. The office then switches to quarterly, anchored in April, still in arrears. The July run wants a bill for 1 April–30 June. May sits inside that window, so that flat is skipped in July. The October run wants 1 July–30 September. Nothing overlaps that window, so the flat receives a full quarter’s charge for July–September. June was never billed, because June was no longer a billing month, and July could not bill a window that included May.

The office that changes frequency in the middle of a year should look at the last period billed for each flat and, where a month would otherwise be skipped, raise that month by hand.

The same society billed in advance, instead of in arrears, changes only the period. The 5 April bill would cover 1 April 2026 – 30 June 2026. The bill date would still be 5 April, the due date would still be 19 April, and the amount would still be ₹12,000.

What the office should remember

  • Bills are raised early on the invoice generation day, and only in a billing month. The billing start month picks those months. The frequency picks how far apart they are.
  • In arrears, the bill covers the months just finished. In advance, it covers the months just starting. The bill date is the generation day either way.
  • The amount is the full frequency: rate per square foot × area × the number of months, plus GST, plus the extra charges on the unit type for that same number of months.
  • A flat that already has a bill overlapping that period is skipped. Closing an allotment does not, by itself, stop the next bill. A moving-in stay is not a separate bill.
  • The due date is the bill date plus the days to pay. The bill becomes overdue the next day, if it is still unpaid or only partly paid.
  • A flat late fee and a percentage late fee are added once, on the first overdue morning. A percentage is of the original bill total. A daily late fee is that day’s amount, each morning the bill stays open.
  • A change in the settings applies to the next run. A missed generation day is not repeated the following day.
