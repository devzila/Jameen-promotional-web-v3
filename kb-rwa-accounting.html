OPERATING GUIDE

How Jameen keeps the society’s accounts

What Jameen records when a bill is raised, when GST is added, when a resident pays, when a charge is waived, when a resident pays more than the bill — and the record an auditor can follow afterwards.

This guide is for the managing committee, the treasurer, and the society’s accountant. It explains what Jameen records when a bill is raised, when GST is added, when a resident pays, when a charge is waived, and when a resident pays more than the bill. It also explains the record an auditor can follow afterwards.

Jameen keeps double-entry books. Every amount that goes into one account comes out of another. Nothing is deleted from the ledger. A mistake is corrected by a reversing entry that points back to the original.

The society’s financial year is the Indian year: 1 April to 31 March.

This is an operating guide for the books inside Jameen. It is not a GST return, and it does not replace the society’s chartered accountant. Where a bill shows GST, the tax figure is stored on the bill. The ledger itself posts the bill total, including that GST, to the income account. Jameen does not keep a separate Output GST liability account, and it does not file GSTR-1.

The accounts you will see

Jameen opens a standard chart of accounts for the society. The ones that matter day to day are these.

AccountWhat it means
Resident ReceivableMoney members still owe on maintenance and other unit bills
Other Income ReceivableMoney owed by someone who is not a member, such as an advertiser
Cash in HandCash the office has received and not yet banked
Bank AccountsMoney received by cheque, UPI, NEFT, RTGS, or IMPS
Maintenance and other income accountsIncome for the charge named on the bill
Late Fee IncomeInterest or late charges added after the bill
Resident AdvancesA temporary holding account used when a waiver is issued, then cleared when that waiver is applied to the same bill
Unallocated ReceiptsMoney received that is more than the bill. It stays here until the society applies it to a later bill or reverses the receipt
Accounts PayableVendor bills the society has approved and not yet paid
Expense accountsElectricity, repairs, and the other costs the society records

A member bill uses Resident Receivable. A bill to an outside party uses Other Income Receivable.

Cash receipts increase Cash in Hand. Every other receipt — cheque, recorded UPI, NEFT, RTGS, and IMPS — increases Bank Accounts.

Raising a bill

A maintenance bill is raised for an allotment, either on the society’s billing day or by hand. Each bill has its own number. The number can include the year or the financial year if the society has set the invoice prefix that way.

On the bill the resident sees:

  • the charge, before tax
  • GST, if the society has set a GST rate on the charge
  • the total, which is charge plus GST

In the books, Jameen posts one journal on the bill date:

  • Debit Resident Receivable for the total
  • Credit the income account chosen for that charge, for the same total

GST is visible on the bill and in the bill register. It is not posted to its own ledger account. The income account therefore contains the gross amount, tax included.

Until the resident pays, the unpaid total sits in Resident Receivable. The bill status moves from pending to partial when some of it is received, and to paid when the bill and any late-fee notes on it are fully settled.

A bill to a non-member, such as advertisement income, is the same idea with Other Income Receivable instead of Resident Receivable.

GST

Use this section when the society charges GST on maintenance or on other supplies.

On the bill. Each line can carry a GST rate. The printed bill shows the charge, the GST, and the line total. The amount the resident must pay is the total, not the charge alone.

In the ledger. The journal uses that total. If a bill is ₹10,000 plus ₹1,800 GST, Resident Receivable and Maintenance Income both move by ₹11,800. There is no separate credit to an Output GST account.

What this means for the society. The bill register is the list of tax collected on invoices. The profit and loss account will show income inclusive of that GST. A chartered accountant who prepares GSTR-1 needs the bill register (taxable value and GST), not a separate GST ledger inside Jameen.

A waiver does not prepare a statutory GST credit note. Waiving part of a bill reduces the income account and the receivable by the waived amount. It does not split that waiver into “taxable value reduced” and “GST reversed,” and it does not produce a Section 34 credit note for the GST return. If the society must report a reduction in taxable value, the accountant prepares that credit note from the bill and the waiver record.

An extra payment is not a GST event. Money paid above the bill does not reduce the tax on the original bill and does not create a tax credit note.

Late fee

If a bill remains unpaid, the society can add a debit note for the late fee. That note is part of the same bill for collection, and it has its own amount still outstanding.

The journal is:

  • Debit Resident Receivable
  • Credit Late Fee Income

A payment is applied to the bill first and then to the open late-fee notes on that bill. The resident’s balance for the bill is the unpaid bill plus any open late fees.

Recording a payment

The office records money already received. The methods are cash, cheque, UPI, NEFT / RTGS / IMPS, and an existing credit note. Each method asks for the details of that instrument: a cash receipt number, cheque number and bank, UPI reference and VPAs, or the UTR and the remitter’s bank and last four account digits.

When the receipt is completed, Jameen:

  1. Allocates the money to the bill, and then to late fees on that bill, up to what is still due.
  2. Posts the journal.
  3. Writes an audit event for the payment and for the allocation.
  4. If anything is left over, creates an overpayment credit note for that leftover. That step is described below. It does not post a second journal.

A receipt that exactly matches the amount due:

  • Debit Cash in Hand or Bank Accounts
  • Credit Resident Receivable

The bill’s received amount increases. A waiver is stored separately as waived amount, so the society can see how much was collected in cash and how much was given up.

One receipt can be split across several bills of the same allotment. It cannot be split across two different flats, or across a member bill and a non-member bill.

Waiving a charge

A waiver is a reduction of one bill. The office enters the amount and a reason. The amount cannot be more than what is still outstanding, and a bill can have only one active waiver.

Jameen does two things in the same step.

Issue the credit note. The note is numbered from the society’s credit-note series and is tied to that bill.

  • Debit the income account of the bill (income goes down)
  • Credit Resident Advances

Apply the credit note to that same bill.

  • Debit Resident Advances
  • Credit Resident Receivable

Resident Advances ends at zero for this waiver. The net effect is: income is lower, and the resident owes less, by the waived amount. The waived amount is taken off the outstanding total. It is not a separate GST calculation.

The bill stores this as waived amount, not as money received. Reports that show collections do not treat a waiver as cash.

A waiver is not kept for a future bill. It is used up on the bill it was raised against.

When the resident pays more than the bill

If the receipt is larger than the bill and its late fees, Jameen keeps the extra money. It does not reduce the original bill below zero, and it does not apply the extra to the next bill by itself.

Example. The bill is ₹10,000 and the late fee is ₹500. The resident pays ₹12,000 by cheque.

  • ₹10,500 is allocated to the bill and the late fee. Both are then settled.
  • ₹1,500 remains.

The receipt journal is:

  • Debit Bank Accounts ₹12,000
  • Credit Resident Receivable ₹10,500
  • Credit Unallocated Receipts ₹1,500

Jameen also creates an overpayment credit note for ₹1,500, linked to that receipt. Creating the note does not post another journal. The ₹1,500 is already in Unallocated Receipts. The credit note is the office record of that balance: who it belongs to, which receipt created it, and how much is still unused.

The next maintenance bill is raised in full. Nobody has to remember a “credit” for the journal to be right, and the new bill is not silently reduced.

To use the ₹1,500, the office records a payment on the later bill and chooses that credit note. The journal is then:

  • Debit Unallocated Receipts
  • Credit Resident Receivable

for the amount applied. The credit note’s used amount increases. Any remainder stays on the note and in Unallocated Receipts.

At 31 March, whatever is still unused remains in Unallocated Receipts. It is a liability on the balance sheet, flat by flat, until it is applied to a bill or the original receipt is reversed. It is not moved into income just because the year has ended.

An overpayment credit note is not a tax credit note. It is the society holding the member’s money.

Reversing a receipt

A completed receipt can be reversed if it was recorded in error.

If the extra amount was never used, Jameen voids that overpayment credit note, marks the reason as the reversal of that receipt, and posts a reversing journal: the original debits and credits are swapped. The bill becomes outstanding again.

If the extra amount has already been applied to another bill, the receipt cannot be reversed. The later application has to be dealt with first. This stops the books from removing money that has already settled a different bill.

Reversal does not delete the original journal. The original entry is marked reversed, and a new reversal entry points back to it.

Society expenses

A vendor bill is recorded as an expense. It does not affect members’ bills.

When the expense is approved:

  • Debit the expense account
  • Credit Accounts Payable

When the society pays the vendor:

  • Debit Accounts Payable
  • Credit Cash in Hand or Bank Accounts

A credit note cannot be used to pay an expense. Vendor payments use cash, cheque, UPI, or NEFT / RTGS / IMPS.

Closing the year

Periods follow 1 April to 31 March. While a year is open, bills, receipts, waivers, and reversals dated in that year can be posted.

When the committee closes the year, Jameen refuses new journals and reversals that belong to that year. An unallocated receipt from the closed year stays on the balance sheet. It is not wiped off by the close. Applying it later, in the new year, is a new journal in the new year: Unallocated Receipts goes down, and the new bill’s receivable goes down.

The audit trail

An auditor should be able to start from a rupee on the trial balance and walk back to the bill, the receipt, or the credit note, and to the person who recorded it. Jameen keeps three layers.

The document. Every bill, debit note, credit note, receipt, and expense has a number, a date, an amount, and the flat or party it belongs to. A waiver credit note names the bill and the reason. An overpayment credit note names the receipt that created it. A voided credit note keeps the void reason, the time, and the person who voided it.

The journal. Each document posts a balanced journal entry with its own entry number, the property, the date, and a reference back to the document. Lines name the account, the debit or credit, and the party where there is one. Entries are posted. They are not edited to hide a mistake. The correction is a reversal entry dated when the correction is made, linked to the entry it reverses.

The activity log. Beside the journal, Jameen writes an event each time an accounting document is created, posted, applied, voided, or reversed. The event stores:

  • what happened (bill posted, payment allocated, credit note applied, journal reversed, and so on)
  • which document it happened to
  • who did it
  • when it happened
  • the request, and the IP address, when the action came from the office application

Critical actions, such as reversing a bill, a receipt, or a journal, are marked as such. Closing or reopening a financial year is logged too.

Changes to the journal entries themselves are also versioned, so a later edit of a posted record is not silent.

Together, the bill PDF, the credit note, the receipt, the journal register, and the activity log are the audit file. The society does not need a separate spreadsheet to explain a waiver or an extra payment, provided the office uses these screens rather than adjusting income by hand.

A short example

Priya, flat A-101. Opening balances are zero.

DateWhat the office doesWhat the books do
1 SepMaintenance bill INV-001. The printed bill shows the charge and the GST separately. The amount due, and the amount posted, is ₹10,000.Debit Resident Receivable 10,000. Credit Maintenance Income 10,000.
12 SepLate fee ₹500 on INV-001Debit Resident Receivable 500. Credit Late Fee Income 500. She now owes 10,500.
12 SepWaiver of ₹200, reason “leakage adjustment”Income down 200, receivable down 200. Resident Advances nets to zero. She owes 10,300. The 200 is waived, not collected.
13 SepCash ₹3,000, receipt number R-1Debit Cash in Hand 3,000. Credit Resident Receivable 3,000. She owes 7,300.
14 SepCheque ₹8,000Debit Bank 8,000. Credit Resident Receivable 7,300. Credit Unallocated Receipts 700. An overpayment credit note for ₹700 is created. INV-001 and the late fee are settled.
1 OctNext bill INV-002 for ₹2,000Debit Resident Receivable 2,000. Credit the income account 2,000. The ₹700 is not applied. She owes 2,000, and the society still holds ₹700.
5 OctOffice applies the ₹700 credit note to INV-002Debit Unallocated Receipts 700. Credit Resident Receivable 700. She owes 1,300.

After 5 October the trial balance still shows the cash and the bank, the income net of the waiver, the late fee, and a receivable of ₹1,300. Unallocated Receipts is back to zero because the ₹700 was applied. If the office had not applied it, Unallocated Receipts would still show ₹700 at the year end.

What the office should remember

  • The figure on the bill is the figure in Resident Receivable. GST is part of that figure, not a second ledger.
  • A waiver reduces income and the amount owed. It is not cash collected, and it is not a GST return by itself.
  • Money paid above the bill sits in Unallocated Receipts and on an overpayment credit note. The next bill is not reduced until someone applies that note.
  • That unused balance survives the year end.
  • Corrections are reversals. They name the original entry and the person who reversed it.
