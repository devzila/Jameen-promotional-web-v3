How the helpdesk works

How a maintenance request is logged, who it is given to, how long the society has to answer and finish it, and how it is closed.

This guide is for the managing committee, the office, the technicians, and residents who raise a complaint. It explains how a maintenance request is logged, who it is given to, how long the society has to answer and to finish it, and how it is closed.

A request is one complaint or one job. It can be about a flat or about a common asset such as a lift, a pump, or the fire system. Assets are set up with the property, as described in How to onboard a property on Jameen.

Each request gets a number from the society’s name. Suncity Parikrama starts at SP-1. Horizon Residency starts at HR-1. The number is unique inside that society.

Who does what

PersonWhat they do
ResidentRaises a complaint for their flat, adds a photo, writes updates, and confirms when the work is done. They can also reopen it if the problem is still there.
SecurityLogs a complaint for a flat from the gate.
OfficeLogs a complaint on a resident’s behalf, assigns a technician or a vendor, watches the time limits, and closes or cancels the job.
TechnicianAcknowledges the job, starts work, pauses it, and marks it resolved.
VendorA contractor on an annual contract, on call, or in house. The office assigns the job to the vendor in the same way as to a technician.

A new society is given a standard set of categories: plumbing, electrical, civil and carpentry, housekeeping, lift, fire and safety, security and access, common-area air conditioning, preventive servicing, and general. Each category has smaller headings under it, such as leakage or a blocked drain. A heading can be switched off. A new complaint takes its priority from the heading, unless the person logging it chooses a different priority.

Raising a complaint

A complaint needs a title and a category. The rest can be added with it.

FieldWhat to enter
TitleWhat is wrong, in one line. Required.
DescriptionThe detail: which room, since when, what was already tried.
CategoryThe heading, such as leakage or lift breakdown.
Flat or assetA flat complaint is tied to the flat. A common-area complaint is tied to an asset. Tying it to an asset marks it as a common area.
WhereA short note of the place, if the flat or asset name is not enough.
When someone is homeA date and a time the resident can let the technician in.
PriorityLow, normal, high, or urgent. If nobody sets it, the category’s priority is used.
PhotoOne photo, up to 1 MB. It has to be a picture.

Who logged it is stored on the complaint.

Raised fromWhat it means
Resident appThe resident sent it.
GateSecurity logged it for a flat.
OfficeThe office logged it. The resident is told that a complaint was raised for the flat.
PreventiveJameen raised it from a servicing schedule.

The resident, the maintenance team, and, when the office raised it, the resident of the flat are notified when the complaint is created. If the resident has an email address, they are also emailed. Assigning a technician later notifies that technician.

A voice recording can be sent from the resident app, with an optional flat and a place note. The recording is kept, up to 5 MB, until it is turned into a complaint. A recording that is still waiting, or that has failed, can be cancelled by the resident or the office. Sending a recording does not, by itself, open a numbered complaint yet.

The life of a complaint

A complaint moves through a fixed set of stages. A stage can be skipped only along the paths below. A closed complaint and a cancelled complaint do not move again.

StageWhat it means
LoggedThe complaint is in. Nobody has taken it yet. This is where a new complaint starts.
AcknowledgedSomeone has accepted it. This is the moment the response time is met.
AssignedA technician or a vendor has been named.
In progressWork has started.
On holdWork is paused. The reason is waiting for the resident, waiting for parts, or waiting for a vendor visit.
ResolvedThe office or the technician says the work is done. The resident has not confirmed yet.
ReopenedThe resident says it is not done. The job is open again.
ClosedThe resident confirmed, or the confirmation window ran out.
CancelledThe complaint was withdrawn. It is left out of the time-limit results.

How it moves:

FromWhat can happen next
LoggedAcknowledge, assign, start work, mark resolved, or cancel.
AcknowledgedAssign, start work, mark resolved, or cancel.
AssignedStart work, put on hold, assign to someone else, mark resolved, or cancel.
In progressPut on hold, assign, mark resolved, or cancel.
On holdResume work, which returns it to in progress, or mark resolved, or cancel.
ResolvedThe resident closes it, or reopens it. The office can do either as well.
ReopenedAcknowledge, assign, start work, or cancel. The resolution time starts again.

A draft is a complaint that has been captured and not yet confirmed as a real job. The response and resolution clocks do not start on a draft. Confirming it moves it to logged and starts those clocks. Ordinary complaints from the resident app, the gate, and the office are logged immediately. They are not drafts.

Every change of stage is written on the complaint as a public update: the time, and the stage it moved to.

How long the society has

Two clocks are set when the complaint is logged.

  • Response. The time allowed before someone acknowledges the complaint.
  • Resolution. The time allowed before the work is marked resolved. It is counted from the acknowledgement, or from the moment the complaint was logged if it has not been acknowledged.

The clocks follow the priority. A new company is given these limits.

PriorityAcknowledge withinResolve withinClock
Normal4 hours48 hoursOffice hours
High2 hours24 hoursOffice hours
Urgent30 minutes8 hoursAll day, including weekends
Low8 hours4 daysOffice hours, when no separate rule has been set

Office hours start as Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00. A complaint logged at 17:00 on Friday with a 4-hour response does not become late at 21:00 that night. The remaining time runs on Monday morning. Holidays the society has listed are skipped in the same way. Urgent work does not wait for Monday.

The society can replace these with its own rules. A rule can be for one category and one priority, for a category, for a priority, or for the company as a whole. Jameen uses the most specific rule that is switched on. The clocks are fixed on the complaint when it is logged. Changing the rule later does not rewrite a complaint already open.

A complaint is late when the response time has passed and nobody has acknowledged it, or when the resolution time has passed and it is not yet resolved. The office is notified when it first becomes late. Cancelled, resolved, and closed complaints are not watched.

Pausing the clock

Putting the job on hold can pause the resolution clock. The pause counts when the reason is waiting for the resident, waiting for parts, or waiting for a vendor visit. Those are the reasons a new company starts with. The minutes spent on hold are added back onto the resolution deadline when work resumes. A hold for another reason does not add that pause.

After a reopen

By default, reopening starts a fresh resolution clock from the category and priority rules. The breach mark is cleared. The society can instead choose to keep only the time that was still left. That choice is a company setting.

When nobody has moved it

Jameen checks open complaints every few minutes against the escalation list. A new company starts with three steps. Each step fires once.

StepWhenWho is notified
1The response time has passed and the complaint is still not acknowledgedThe society office
2The resolution time has passed and the work is not resolvedThe assignee’s supervisor
3Four hours have passed since the last update on the complaintA committee member

The notice is sent in the app. A step that has already fired is not sent again.

Updates, notes, and ready replies

People write on the complaint in two ways.

  • An update is the conversation. Residents see updates that are not marked internal. A status change is one of these. A resident can add their own update.
  • An internal note is for the office and the technician. Residents do not see it.

A comment is a shorter remark on the complaint. The resident, the office, and the technician can each leave one.

The company is given ready replies for the moments that repeat: the complaint has been logged, a technician has been assigned, a vendor visit is booked, work is waiting on parts, and the work is done and needs the resident’s confirmation. They are text the office can send. They are not sent on their own.

Assigning the work

The office assigns the complaint to a technician or to a vendor. The assignment records who assigned it, when, and why. Assigning again moves the current assignment on. The new person is notified.

A vendor is a party the society already keeps as a vendor or a contractor, plus a maintenance profile: annual contract, on call, or in house, the categories they cover, and the date their contract ends. One party has one profile.

While an annual contract is inside 30 days of its end, the office is reminded. The same reminder is sent for an asset whose own annual-contract date is inside 30 days. That asset date is entered when the asset is set up.

Marking it done, and closing it

When the work is finished, the technician or the office marks the complaint resolved. The resident is asked to confirm. They can:

  • Close it, with a satisfaction rating from 1 to 5 and an optional comment. The complaint is then closed. If a vendor was assigned, that vendor’s rating is recalculated from the ratings on their jobs.
  • Reopen it, if the problem is still there. The complaint goes back to reopened and the resolution clock starts again.

If the resident does neither, Jameen closes the complaint 48 hours after it was marked resolved. The society can change that number of hours.

A complaint can be closed only from resolved. It cannot be closed straight from in progress.

Charging the cost

When the complaint is closed, a cost entered on it is posted once.

  • If the office marked the cost as payable by the resident, Jameen adds a debit note for that amount on the flat’s latest bill that is still open. The reason names the complaint number. If that flat has no open bill, the charge is not posted.
  • If the cost stays with the society, Jameen records an expense for the same amount, against the vendor when one is assigned.

A cost of zero is ignored. A complaint that already has an expense or a resident charge is not charged a second time. How a debit note and an expense sit in the books is described in How Jameen keeps the society’s accounts.

Planned servicing

A society can keep a repeating job: a title, a category, how often it runs, an optional asset, and an optional vendor. The frequencies are weekly, monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, and yearly.

A new property is given one such job: monthly pump and motor servicing, due about a month after the property is created. Each morning Jameen opens a complaint for every job that is due, marks it as preventive, and assigns the vendor when one was named. The next due date then moves forward by the frequency.

Switch the schedule off and no further complaints are opened from it. Complaints already opened stay.

What the resident sees

The resident’s open list is everything still in motion, including a complaint marked resolved and waiting for their confirmation. Their history is resolved, closed, and cancelled work.

They can cancel a complaint that has not been resolved or closed. They cannot cancel a closed one.

On the home screen, open complaints are listed with the latest community updates. Community updates themselves are described in How community updates work.

A short example

Priya in A-101 reports a dripping tap on Monday at 10:00.

WhenWhat happens
Monday 10:00She sends a complaint, category Leakage, one photo. The number is HR-14. Priority is high, from that category. She is notified that it was submitted. The office has 2 office hours to acknowledge and 24 office hours to resolve.
Monday 11:00The office acknowledges it and assigns the plumber. The plumber is notified.
Monday 15:00The plumber starts work, then puts it on hold, reason “waiting for parts”. The resolution clock pauses.
Tuesday 11:00The part arrives. The plumber resumes. The hours on hold are added back to the deadline.
Tuesday 16:00The plumber marks it resolved and enters a cost of ₹800, payable by the resident. Priya is asked to confirm.
Tuesday 18:00She rates it 5 and closes it. A debit note of ₹800 is added to her latest open maintenance bill. The plumber’s vendor rating includes this 5.

If she had not answered, the complaint would have closed on its own 48 hours after Tuesday 16:00. The same debit note would then be posted at that automatic close, because the cost was already on the complaint.

If the tap were still dripping, she would reopen it instead. HR-14 would be open again, with a new resolution clock, and the ₹800 would not be posted until a later close.

What the office should remember

  • A complaint is logged against a flat or a common asset, under a category. The category sets the priority unless someone overrides it.
  • Acknowledging meets the response time. Marking it resolved meets the resolution time. Urgent work runs all day. Other work uses office hours, Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00, unless the society has changed that.
  • On hold pauses the resolution clock only for the three waiting reasons: the resident, parts, or a vendor visit.
  • Residents confirm a resolved complaint with a rating, or they reopen it. Silence for 48 hours closes it.
  • A cost is posted once, at close: to the resident’s open bill when it is chargeable, otherwise as a society expense.
  • Internal notes stay off the resident’s screen. Status changes do not.
  • Planned servicing opens its own complaints. It does not wait for a resident to write in.
