How to onboard a property on Jameen

Every field on the property, and the records under it: towers, unit types, extra charges, flats, parking, security posts, common assets — and the order to set a site up.

This guide is for the person setting up a society, a commercial complex, or a mixed site on Jameen. It explains every field on the property, and then the records that sit under it: towers, unit types, extra charges, flats, parking, security posts, and common assets.

Create the property first. Towers, unit types, flats, parking, and posts all belong to that property. A flat cannot be created until its tower and its unit type exist.

A property is one society or one site. The company above it can hold more than one property. Billing, bank details, towers, and flats are all per property. They are not shared with the next society on the same company.

How a bill is raised from this setup is described in How Jameen raises recurring maintenance bills. How those bills sit in the books is described in How Jameen keeps the society’s accounts.

Fields marked Required must be filled before the record can be saved. The others can be left blank and filled later. Counts of towers, unit types, flats, parking bays, and assets are kept by Jameen as you add those records. You do not type those counts yourself.

The order to set a site up

  1. Create the property: name, address, and city, then the billing and bank details.
  2. Add each tower or block.
  3. Add each unit type, with its area and maintenance rate, and any extra charge that belongs to that type.
  4. Add each flat, pointing it at one tower and one unit type.
  5. Add parking bay numbers.
  6. Add the gate and any other security posts. Each tower already receives a post of its own.
  7. Add common assets if the helpdesk will track them.
  8. Allot flats to owners. A flat with no allotment is not on the scheduled maintenance run.

The property

Name and what kind of site it is

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
NameYesThe society’s name, as it should appear on bills and at the gate. “Suncity Parikrama”, “Horizon Residency”.
Short nameNoA compact name where the full name does not fit.
UseNoResidential, commercial, or mixed. A new property starts as residential. This describes the site. It does not set the maintenance rate. The rate sits on the unit type.
Managed byNoWho runs the site day to day: the association, a builder, or a managing agency. Free text.
Year builtNoThe year the site was built. This is the site’s year, not the year of one flat.

Jameen also builds a two-letter prefix from the name for maintenance request numbers. A two-word name uses the first letter of each of the first two words, so Suncity Parikrama becomes SP. A one-word name uses its first two letters, so Ansal becomes AN. Request numbers then run SP-1, SP-2, and so on. Renaming the property later does not change a prefix already set.

Bank and tax numbers

These are the society’s own details. They are stored with the property so the office and the resident can see where to pay. Recording them does not open a bank feed, and it does not file a GST return.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
Bank nameNoThe bank that holds the society’s account.
BranchNoThe branch.
Account numberNoThe society’s account number.
IFSCNoThe branch IFSC.

Cheque, UPI, NEFT, RTGS, and IMPS receipts are posted to the one Bank Accounts ledger. Cash receipts are posted to Cash in Hand. The bank name and account number identify the society’s bank. They do not split the ledger into one book per account. That split is described in How to read the accounting reports.

Billing fields on the property

These fields decide the scheduled maintenance bill for every flat in the property. One flat cannot have a different generation day or a different late-fee rule. The full cycle, including which months are billing months and when a bill becomes overdue, is in the recurring-bills guide. The meaning of each field is here so it can be filled in at setup.

A new property starts ready to bill: monthly, in arrears, billing month April, generation day the 1st, 14 days to pay, and no late-fee rule chosen.

FieldWhat you are choosingHow a new property starts
Billing frequencyHow often a bill is raised, and how many months of maintenance one bill contains. Monthly, every two months, quarterly, half-yearly, or yearly.Monthly
Billing start monthThe anchor month. April means a quarterly society is billed in April, July, October, and January. The start month does not change the dates printed on the bill.April
In arrears or in advanceIn arrears, the bill covers the months just finished. In advance, it covers the months just starting.In arrears
Invoice generation dayThe day of the month the bills are dated and raised. Use 1 to 31. A day of 29, 30, or 31 falls on the last day of a shorter month.1
Days to payThe number of days after the bill date before the bill is due. The bill becomes overdue the day after the due date.14
Late-fee ruleNone, a flat amount once, a percentage of the bill total once, or an amount each morning the bill stays open.Not set, so no late fee is added
Late-fee valueThe rupee amount, the percent, or the rupee amount per day, matching the rule above.Blank

The maintenance GST rate stored for the society starts at 0%. The GSTIN does not change that rate. Extra charges on a unit type carry their own GST percent, which you set on the charge.

Two figures on the property are stored and are not what the morning runs use.

  • Overdue charge amount is a stored amount. The late fee that is added comes from the late-fee rule and the late-fee value.
  • Automatic invoices, automatic reminders, and days before a reminder are saved on the property. The morning bill run does not read them. Bills are raised while the society is active, on the generation day, in a billing month. Outgoing email, below, is what allows the bill PDF to be sent.
FieldWhat it doesHow a new property starts
Outgoing emailWhen this is on, raising a member bill emails the resident a PDF. When it is off, the email is not sent.On

Set the frequency, the start month, arrears or advance, and the generation day before the first billing day. A generation day that has already passed this month is not repeated tomorrow.

Bill numbers and credit-note numbers

FieldWhat to enterHow a new property starts
Invoice number prefixThe text in front of each bill number.Blank, so the number is just the sequence
Last invoice numberThe last number already used. The next bill takes the next number.0, so the first bill is 1
Credit-note prefixThe text in front of each credit-note number.Blank
Last credit-note numberThe last credit-note number already used.0, so the first credit note is 1

The prefix can include a date token. The token is filled in on the day the document is numbered.

TokenBecomesExample on 15 July 2026
%YEAR%The calendar year2026
%YEAR-MONTH%Year and month2026-07
%FINANCIAL_YEAR%The Indian financial year, 1 April to 31 March2026-27

A prefix of INV-%FINANCIAL_YEAR%- on 15 July 2026 produces a bill such as INV-2026-27-1. On 15 February 2027 the same prefix produces INV-2026-27-, because February is still that financial year. On 1 April 2027 it becomes INV-2027-28-.

The last-number field is the last number issued, not the next one. If the society’s old books ended at 240, set the last invoice number to 240. The next Jameen bill is 241, with the prefix in front.

Switching a property off

A new property is active. Scheduled bills are raised only while it stays active.

FieldWhat it records
Activated onThe date and time the property was switched on.
Deactivation reasonWhy the property was taken down.
Removed onThe date and time it was taken off the active list.

Taking the property off the active list stops the morning bill run for that society. It does not delete bills, receipts, or flats already stored.

Towers and blocks

A tower is a building on the property. Flats belong to a tower. The gate list shows the tower name with the flat number.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
NameGive every tower a nameThe name residents and guards use: “Tower A”, “Block 3”, “Wing B”.
DescriptionNoAnything the office wants beside the name: the phase, the entrance, the number of floors.

Saving a tower creates a security post of the kind Tower, with the same name. Renaming the tower renames that post. You do not add the tower post by hand.

A tower with no name is still saved. Its post is then named from its internal number, which is a poor name at the gate. Name the tower before you add flats.

The same flat number may exist in two towers. Uniqueness is the number inside one tower, not across the society. Tower A flat 101 and Tower B flat 101 are two flats.

Unit types

A unit type is a class of flat that shares an area and a maintenance rate. “2 BHK”, “3 BHK”, and “Shop” are unit types. Every flat of that type is billed from the type’s rate and area.

The name must be unique on the property. Two types cannot both be called “2 BHK”.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
NameYesThe name residents will see on the rate, such as “2 BHK” or “Office”.
DescriptionNoA note about the type.
UseYesResidential or commercial. A unit type does not have a mixed choice. A mixed property uses residential types and commercial types side by side.
Area (square feet)YesThe area this type is billed on. Every flat of this type uses this area. A single flat cannot have its own area.
Maintenance rate per square footYesThe monthly rate. One month of maintenance is this rate times the area. A quarterly bill multiplies that month by three. The frequency on the property decides the multiplier.

Example. Rate ₹4 per square foot, area 1,000. One month is ₹4,000. On a monthly frequency the maintenance line is ₹4,000. On a quarterly frequency it is ₹12,000. GST on that line uses the society’s maintenance GST rate, which starts at 0%.

Changing the rate or the area changes the next scheduled bill. It does not rewrite a bill already raised.

Extra charges on a unit type

An extra charge is a line added to every scheduled bill for flats of that type: parking, club, sinking fund, and similar heads. The name must be unique on that unit type.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
NameYesThe line name on the bill.
DescriptionNoA note for the office.
KindYesFixed, or a percentage of maintenance.
Monthly amountYesFor a fixed charge, the rupee amount per month. For a percentage charge, the percent of the maintenance amount, before GST.
GST percentYesThe GST rate on this line only. It can be 0.

A fixed charge of ₹500 a month is ₹500 on a monthly bill and ₹1,500 on a quarterly bill. A charge of 10% is 10% of the maintenance amount for that same number of months, before GST. GST is then this charge’s own percent.

An extra charge that is switched off is left off the next bill. Charges start active.

Flats

A flat is one unit. It must point at a tower and a unit type. Those two links are how Jameen knows where the flat is and what to charge.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
Flat numberYesThe door number, unique inside that tower. “101”, “A-1204”, “G-02”.
TowerYesThe tower this flat is in.
Unit typeYesThe type whose area and rate will be billed.
Year builtYesThe year of this flat. Jameen requires it even when it matches the society’s year.
BedroomsNoA count, for the office record.
BathroomsNoA count, for the office record.
Electricity accountNoThe electricity board account number for this flat.
Water accountNoThe water account number for this flat.
Internal extensionNoAn intercom or extension number.

A new flat starts unallotted. It has no owner on it, and the scheduled maintenance run does not bill it.

The flat then moves through three states:

StateWhat it means
UnallottedNo current allotment. Not on the scheduled bill run.
VacantAllotted, and nobody has moved in.
OccupiedAllotted, and a moving-in stay is current.

The scheduled bill follows the allotment, not the moving-in stay. Create the allotment when the flat should start receiving maintenance bills. The date the allotment started does not reduce the first bill to a part-month. The first billing day charges the full frequency. That rule is in the recurring-bills guide.

Closing an allotment does not, by itself, stop the next scheduled bill. Plan that before you end an allotment.

Parking

A parking record is a bay number on the property. Creating it does not attach it to a flat. Attaching it is a later step, and it needs a flat that already has a current allotment.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
Parking numberYesThe bay number, unique across the whole property. “B1-014”, “OPEN-22”. Two towers cannot use the same bay number.

A bay is either allotted or free.

  • Free means no current link to an allotment.
  • Allotted means it is linked to a current allotment. The flat on that allotment is the flat the bay belongs to.

A vehicle can then be placed on an allotted bay. The bay number itself stays the same. Ending the link returns the bay to free. The bay record remains, so the number can be allotted again.

A fixed parking charge on the unit type is a rupee line on the maintenance bill. It is not this bay list. The bay list is which physical bay belongs to which flat. The charge is what the bill asks for. Set both if the society does both.

Security posts

A security post is a place a guard can be on duty: a gate, a tower lobby, the clubhouse, a parking entrance, or another point. Shifts are opened against a post. A guard’s open shift is what lets the gate record visitors.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
NameYesThe name the guard sees: “Main Gate”, “Tower A”, “Clubhouse”.
KindYesTower, gate, clubhouse, parking, or other.
TowerOnly for a tower postThe tower this lobby belongs to. A gate, clubhouse, parking, or other post has no tower.
ActiveNoA new post is active. An inactive post remains on the list and is not a place to open a new shift.
PositionNoA whole number used to sort the list. Lower numbers come first, then the name. A new post starts at 0.

Tower posts are created for you. Adding a tower adds one tower post with that tower’s name, and only one tower post can point at a given tower. Rename the tower to rename the post. Add a tower post by hand only when you are tying a post to a tower that does not already have one.

Add the other posts yourself. A typical site needs at least one gate post, because that is where visitors are received. Clubhouse, parking, and other are extra posts if those points have their own guard.

A post that already has a shift cannot be removed until that history is dealt with. Plan the post list before guards start opening shifts.

Common assets

Assets are the plant and common equipment the helpdesk can attach a complaint to: a lift, a generator, a pump, CCTV, the fire system. They are not flats, and they are not billed to residents from this screen.

FieldRequiredWhat to enter
NameYesThe asset’s name: “Lift 2, Tower A”, “DG set”.
DescriptionNoWhat it is, or a serial number, in words.
TypeNoCommon area, equipment, lift, generator, pump, gate, CCTV, water tank, fire system, or other.
LocationNoWhere it stands.
Installed onNoThe installation date.
Warranty endsNoThe warranty end date.
AMC vendorNoThe contractor who holds the annual maintenance contract, if that vendor is already on the society.
AMC endsNoThe date that contract ends. Jameen can remind the office when an AMC is inside 30 days of this date.

A new asset starts active. It can later be inactive, or under maintenance.

Documents can be attached to the asset: a warranty card, an AMC copy, a manual. Each document has a name, a description, and a file.

A short example

Horizon Residency, a residential society in Pune.

StepWhat the office enters
PropertyName Horizon Residency. Address and city filled. Use residential. Quarterly, start month April, in arrears, generation day 5, 14 days to pay, no late fee yet. Invoice prefix HR-%FINANCIAL_YEAR%-, last number 0. Bank, IFSC, and GSTIN filled. Outgoing email left on.
TowersTower A and Tower B. Each save creates a tower security post of the same name.
Unit types2 BHK, residential, 980 square feet, ₹4 per square foot. 3 BHK, residential, 1,450 square feet, ₹4 per square foot.
Extra chargeOn 2 BHK, a fixed charge named Parking, ₹500 a month, GST 0.
FlatsA-101 on Tower A, type 2 BHK, year 2014. B-201 on Tower B, type 3 BHK, year 2014. Both start unallotted.
ParkingBays B1-014 and B1-015. Both start free.
PostsA post named Main Gate, kind gate. The two tower posts already exist.
AssetLift 1, Tower A, type lift, AMC end date filled.

A-101 is still unallotted, so 5 April raises nothing for it. After the office allots A-101, the next quarterly billing morning raises one bill: maintenance ₹4 × 980 × 3 months, plus parking ₹500 × 3, dated the 5th, due 14 days later. The bill number starts HR-2026-27-1 if that morning falls in financial year 2026-27.

What the office should remember

  • Name, address, and city are the only fields a property cannot be saved without. Billing can be corrected later, and it applies to bills not yet raised.
  • The billing frequency, start month, arrears or advance, generation day, days to pay, and late-fee rule are society-wide. The rate and the area are on the unit type.
  • Add towers and unit types before flats. A flat number is unique inside its tower.
  • Saving a tower creates its tower security post. Add the gate post yourself.
  • A parking bay is only a number until it is linked to a current allotment. A parking charge on the unit type is a separate line on the bill.
  • A flat is billed on the scheduled run after it has an allotment. The first bill is the full period, not a part-month.
  • Outgoing email controls the bill PDF. The automatic-invoice and reminder switches are stored, and the morning bill run does not read them.
