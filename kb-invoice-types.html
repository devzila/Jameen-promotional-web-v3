The three kinds of member bills

Scheduled maintenance, one-off custom bills, and formula-based template bills — when to use each, and how collection works after they are raised.

On this page

  1. Which bill to use
  2. Scheduled bills
  3. Custom bills
  4. Template bills
  5. After any of the three is raised
  6. A short example
  7. What the office should remember

This guide is for the managing committee, the treasurer, and the office. Jameen raises a member bill in three ways. Each way is for a different kind of charge. After the bill exists, collection is the same: a due date, an overdue morning, a late fee if the society has set one, and a receipt against Resident Receivable.

How a scheduled maintenance bill chooses its month, its period, and its late fee is described in How Jameen raises recurring maintenance bills. How the bill is posted in the books is described in How Jameen keeps the society’s accounts.

A bill to someone who is not a member, such as an advertiser, is not one of these three. It is raised on its own and does not use the maintenance rate or these templates.

All three use the society’s bill-number series. A prefix such as HR-%FINANCIAL_YEAR%- applies to a scheduled bill, a custom bill, and an electricity bill alike.

Scheduled bills

Use a scheduled bill for the charge the society already knows how to calculate and wants raised without typing it again. That is the maintenance rate per square foot, the area of the unit type, and any extra charge on that unit type, for the number of months in the billing frequency.

Jameen raises these early on the invoice generation day, and only in a billing month. The office does not pick the flats one by one. Every allotment on the property is included. A flat with no allotment is not included. A moving-in stay is not a separate bill.

The amount is the full period. A resident who joined in the last month of a quarter still receives the full quarter. The printed period is the months just finished when the society bills in arrears, or the months just starting when it bills in advance.

The resident is notified, and emailed the PDF when outgoing email is on.

Use this for maintenance, a fixed parking charge on the unit type, a sinking fund set as a percent of maintenance, and any other line that should repeat on the same cycle. Do not use it for a charge that is different for each flat this month, or for a charge that should happen only once.

The billing frequency, the start month, the generation day, the days to pay, and the late fee are society settings. They are explained in the recurring-bills guide. Changing them changes the next scheduled run. It does not rewrite a bill already raised, and it does not change how a custom bill or a template bill is calculated.

Custom bills

Use a custom bill when the office has already decided the amount, and that same bill should go to one flat or to many. Typical uses are a festival contribution, a share of a repair, an ad-hoc deposit adjustment, or a charge for one tower that is not on the unit-type rate card.

The office enters the bill once, then chooses who receives that same bill.

What the office entersWhat it does
FlatsOne running allotment, several, or all of them. A closed allotment is not in “all”.
LinesEach line has a name, a description, an amount, and a GST percent. GST is that percent of the line amount. A percent of zero means no GST.
Income accountWhere the bill is credited. Choose the account that matches the charge, so maintenance and a repair recovery do not land in the same income head.
Bill dateDefaults to today.
Due dateDefaults to the bill date plus the society’s days to pay. Enter a date when this charge should be due sooner or later than maintenance.
PeriodOptional. The from and to dates printed on the bill. They do not have to match a billing month.

Every selected flat receives the same lines and the same total. A custom bill cannot bill A-101 for ₹500 and B-201 for ₹800 in the same run. When the amounts differ, run it twice, or use a template if the difference comes from a formula.

The bills are numbered and posted to Resident Receivable and to the income account chosen. They are raised as a batch. They do not send the new-bill email that a scheduled bill sends. The resident still sees the bill in the app, and it becomes overdue, and can take a late fee, by the same rules as any other member bill.

A custom bill does not look at the maintenance rate, the area, or the billing frequency. It does not wait for the generation day.

Template bills

Use a template when the society repeats a calculation, and the result is different for each flat because a figure differs. The office does not type the rupee total. It fills in the figures the template asks for. Jameen applies the formula, shows the result, and raises the bills when the office confirms.

A template is prepared for the society. It names the formula, the figures that are shared by every flat in the run, the figures that are entered per flat, and the income account. The office does not write a new formula on the bill screen. When the society needs a calculation Jameen does not already provide, a template is added for that calculation and then appears for the office to run.

Jameen currently provides one such template: electricity.

Electricity

Use it when the society recovers electricity from meter readings, rather than as a flat monthly charge on the unit type.

The office prepares one run.

Shared for the whole run

FigureWhat to enter
Rate per unitRequired. The rupee rate for one unit of electricity. Every flat in the run uses this rate.
PeriodThe from and to dates printed on the bill. This is the consumption period, not the society’s maintenance cycle.

Entered for each flat

FigureWhat to enter
Previous readingThe meter reading at the start of the period. Required.
Current readingThe meter reading at the end of the period. Required. It should be the later reading, and it should be higher than the previous reading.
Late-fee surchargeOptional. An amount the office adds on that flat for this bill only.

Jameen then calculates each flat.

  • Units used = current reading minus previous reading.
  • Energy charge = units used × the rate per unit.
  • The line on the bill reads as an electricity bill for those units at that rate.
  • If a late-fee surcharge was entered and it is more than zero, it is a second line on the same bill. It is part of the bill total from the day the bill is raised. It is not the society’s automatic late fee, which can still be added later if this bill goes overdue.
  • This calculation does not add GST. The amount due for the energy is the units times the rate.

Example. The rate is ₹8 a unit. Flat A-101 was 1,200 last month and is 1,350 now. Units used are 150. The energy charge is ₹1,200. If the office also enters a surcharge of ₹50, the bill total is ₹1,250.

The run is a draft until the office confirms it. Confirming raises one bill per flat included in the run, dated that day, due that day plus the society’s days to pay. The income is Electricity Recovery Income. The resident is notified, and emailed the PDF when outgoing email is on.

Confirm once. The same run is not meant to be confirmed again.

A flat left out of the readings is not billed. A scheduled maintenance bill in the same month does not include these units. Electricity stays on this bill, and maintenance stays on the scheduled bill, so the two income accounts stay apart.

If the society would rather charge a fixed electricity amount every month, put that amount on the unit type as an extra charge and let the scheduled bill raise it. Use this template only when the bill must follow the meter.

Adding another template

A further template is added when a society needs another calculated bill of the same kind: one rate or rule for the run, and a figure that changes from flat to flat. Water from meter readings is the usual next case. Until that template is added, a water charge that is the same for every selected flat is a custom bill, and a water charge that is a fixed monthly amount is an extra charge on the unit type.

The office runs a new template the same way as electricity: fill the shared figures, fill each flat, review the calculated amounts, then confirm.

After any of the three is raised

The bill is a member bill. Its number comes from the society’s series. Its outstanding amount sits in Resident Receivable until it is paid, waived, or written off.

The due date is a day the resident may still pay. The bill becomes overdue the next day if it is still unpaid or only partly paid. If the society has a late-fee rule, the morning late-fee run can add a debit note. That rule is the same for a maintenance bill, a custom bill, and an electricity bill. A surcharge typed onto an electricity bill is separate, and it is already part of that bill.

A payment is applied to the bill first and then to open late fees. A waiver reduces that bill only. Money paid above the bill is held as an unallocated receipt and is not subtracted from the next bill of any of these three kinds.

GST, where a bill has it, is inside the total posted to the income account. The electricity template’s energy charge has no GST added by the formula. A custom line has GST only when the office enters a percent. A scheduled line uses the GST percent on the property for maintenance, which starts at zero, and the GST percent on each extra charge.

A short example

Horizon Residency bills maintenance quarterly and electricity from meters.

DateWhat the office doesWhich kind
5 AprilNothing. Jameen raises the quarterly maintenance bills, in arrears, for January to March. A 2 BHK is rate × area × 3 months, plus any extra charge on that unit type.Scheduled
6 AprilThe cultural committee has agreed a ₹1,000 Holi contribution, with no GST, for every running allotment. The office raises one custom bill, income “Other income”, due in 7 days, period left blank.Custom
7 AprilThe electrician brings the meter book. The office opens the electricity template, sets ₹8 a unit and the period 1 March to 31 March, and enters each flat’s previous and current reading. After checking the units, the office confirms. Each flat gets its own electricity bill, due 14 days later, in Electricity Recovery Income.Template

A-101 might then owe three bills: the quarter’s maintenance, ₹1,000 for Holi, and 150 units at ₹8. They are collected separately. Paying one does not reduce the others.

What the office should remember

  • Scheduled bills are the repeating maintenance run. Custom bills are an amount the office types, shared by every flat it selects. Template bills are a formula, different for each flat.
  • Do not put meter readings into a custom bill. The custom amount cannot change from flat to flat in one run.
  • Do not put a one-off festival charge on the unit type if it should happen only once. That would repeat on every future scheduled bill.
  • Electricity is the template Jameen provides. The charge is units used times the rate. Confirming the draft is what raises the bills.
  • All three become ordinary member bills. The due date, the overdue rule, and the society’s late fee apply after that.
